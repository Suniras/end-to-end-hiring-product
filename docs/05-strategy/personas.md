# Personas

**AUTHOR: the frame and the candidate list are Claude's. The choices are Suniras's and the slots are empty.**

Created 27 August 2026, after Suniras decided that one primary persona is the wrong shape for this product
because hiring involves several people and departments.

---

## The decision, and one concern with it

Suniras's direction: three or four main personas rather than one primary user.

**He is right, and this is standard practice for a product sold to a large company.** One person uses it, a
different person signs for it, a third one champions it internally, and a fourth can kill it. Naming only the
user leaves three quarters of the sale undescribed.

**The concern, once, then it is his call.** On 16 August, asked whose screen this opens on, the answer was
"hiring managers, store managers and anyone involved in hiring." That was recorded as three roles plus anyone,
and the note against it said this is how a product gets designed by committee. Four co-equal users is that same
answer wearing better clothes.

**What separates the good version from the bad one is what makes them different from each other.** If the four
are differentiated by their **role in the decision and in the workflow**, it is a proper enterprise persona map.
If they are four people who all use the product roughly equally, it is a committee.

**And one question survives either way.** Four personas or one, something has to open when the product loads.
That is a single answer and it is still owed.

## The four slots

The standard enterprise shape, and it fits this product well because the hiring journey genuinely crosses
departments.

### Slot 1: the primary user

The person whose daily work the product is designed around. Whose screen opens. Whose hours the product gives
back.

**Evidenced candidates:**

**Store manager.** Feels the problem hardest. Twelve to fourteen hour days, with hiring on top of running a
store. Does the screening, the interviewing and the closing. Loses candidates because getting through the work
takes longer than the candidate's patience. Cannot buy anything.

**Field HR business partner.** Owns the hourly hiring process across a group of stores, is the primary HR
contact for them, and supports hiring and onboarding in partnership with central talent acquisition. Titles
vary; Walmart appears to use Market HR Manager. **How many stores one covers is not published by any retailer
and four rounds of research did not find it.** Their own stated measures include hiring, which no other
candidate can say.

**Recruiting operations or central talent acquisition.** Has volume and no authority over any store. This is
the role Joy's material implies, and it is the role most likely to be running Fountain or Paradox today.

### Slot 2: the buyer

The person who signs, or who owns the budget line the money comes from.

**Evidenced candidate:**

**District manager.** Operations rather than HR. Kohl's publishes a span of 15 to 20 stores. Accountable for
driving sales, profit and productivity while meeting payroll, expense and shrink goals, which means **this role
owns the payroll budget**, the constraint store managers actually name. On hiring they hire store managers and
only assist with hourly hiring.

**The problem with this slot, and it is the central adoption risk.** Their stated measures are sales, profit,
productivity, payroll and shrink. Hiring appears there as a cost. A product that fills roles faster does not
obviously improve anything this person is paid on. That is sharper than the store-manager version of the
problem, because it means the operations chain has a reason to be indifferent rather than merely busy.

**One capability now exists that speaks to this role directly.** A comparison of one store's hiring funnel
against comparable stores in the same district is useless to a store manager, who does not run the others, and
is exactly what a district manager does all week. It is built and on screen in the demo.

**Above the district manager**, and unevidenced by anything in this repository: a chief HR officer buys a talent
story, a chief operating officer buys store productivity, a chief financial officer buys a cost line, and a VP
of talent acquisition buys their own team's workload. Four different pitches, and which one applies has never
been established.

### Slot 3: the champion

The person who wants this to exist and will argue for it internally. Often not the buyer and often not the
heaviest user.

**No candidate is evidenced.** This slot is empty because nobody outside Nurix has told us the problem is real.
It is worth naming as empty rather than filling it with a guess, and it is one more reason the practitioner
conversation matters.

### Slot 4: the blocker

The person who can stop the purchase even if everyone else wants it. Naming them early is cheaper than meeting
them late.

**Two are strongly evidenced by the design itself:**

**Legal or employment counsel.** The product scores candidates and a human decides, which makes it a regulated
automated employment decision tool. That brings an annual independent bias audit with a published summary, ten
business days of candidate notice in New York City, disparate impact exposure if the score correlates with
race, sex or age, and a requirement to offer disabled candidates an alternative path. California's rules, in
force since October 2025, count a tool that merely helps a human decide, so keeping a person in the loop does
not avoid the definition there. **This person does not have to be persuaded that hiring is slow. They have to be
persuaded the product will not create a lawsuit.**

**IT security.** The product connects to the retailer's systems and holds identity data. If it stores Social
Security numbers, several states impose affirmative technical duties on the holder, and a large retailer's
security review is not gentle. **This is the review that kills enterprise deals quietly.**

## What to fill in, and the shape of a finished answer

For each slot: the role, one sentence on what they stop doing or start being able to do, and what would make
that choice wrong.

That last part is not decoration. Every other decision in this project carries a reversal condition and it is
the only thing that makes a wrong guess recoverable.

---

## Two things to accept along with four personas

**Four personas is more product, and scope is already wide.** All twenty funnel steps are in scope with no
slice. Adding four distinct people to serve, each with their own view, is a large version 1. The four slots
above mitigate this, because only slot 1 needs a designed workspace. The buyer needs a report, the champion
needs an argument, and the blocker needs an audit trail. **Those are three different artefacts, not three more
products.** Saying that out loud is what keeps four personas from becoming four roadmaps.

**The primary user question does not go away.** Slot 1 still has three candidates and no evidence separating
them. Four personas is a better description of the sale. It is not an answer to whose screen opens.
