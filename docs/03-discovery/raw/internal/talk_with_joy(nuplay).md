
# Colleague Interview Notes — Frontline / Contingent Staffing Software

Rough notes captured from interviewing one colleague who has experimented with
this kind of software previously.

## 1. LinkedIn knowledge / skill graph taxonomy
- Built from JD + years of experience.
- Based on social media to know where they are (currently) working.
- Needs LinkedIn API access.
- Incoming application parser.

## 2. 72-hr close (TAT)
- 72-hour turnaround time to close a role.

## 3. Matching Algo
- JD to graph.
- Resume to graph + (resume parser). `[Simplify VMS — software name]`
- `[RUBRICS]` → % fitness, skill-based weightage.

## 4. USA market dynamics
- JD-to-application ratio is usually not very high.
- Vendors who provide candidates; earning potential is high.
- Cost of processing an application is not high.

## 5. Contingent hiring?
- Beeline (AI startup).
- Reputation of the person who is in the candidate pool.
  - e.g. a nurse doing multiple internships across ~6 months at different
    places — we know their reputation from past work experience, which generates
    credibility.
- Credentialing of such candidates:
  - e.g. attendance record (sincere).
  - intern switches between jobs.

## 6. Insurance & sourcing / commercial model
- (Zenefits & Gusto) insurance providers.
- Direct sourcing (72 hrs?) → hence ~14% commission, not ~8% commission.

## 7. Job board integration
- Not just LinkedIn.
- Europe has heavy job board penetration — 80–90% of sourcing happens through
  job boards.
- Aggregation capability is needed: one hiring company (e.g. a retailer) will
  not take out subscriptions to two, three, or four different job boards, so an
  aggregation layer sources talent across all of them.

## 8. Cost computation engine
- AI call, AI screening, resume parsing, job board integration.
