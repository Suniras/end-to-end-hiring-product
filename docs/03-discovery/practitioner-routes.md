# Ways to reach somebody who actually hires retail staff

Written 24 Aug 2026, after Nishant could not arrange the Target conversation. Route 0 added 25 Aug.

This is a list of options with what each one costs and what it is likely to return. The choice is Suniras's.
Q-001 has been open since 9 August and it is the oldest unmanaged risk in the project.

**Update 25 Aug 2026, and it changes the order.** Two things came out of the call where Nishant relayed his
conversations with Anuj and Joy. Nishant confirmed he has no practitioner connections of his own, so the
internal network is not a slow route, it is an empty one. And a route nobody had noticed appeared: Anuj Jain
met US clients in person a couple of months ago and his PRD came out of those meetings. That is now route 0,
and it should be tried before any of the five below, because it costs one message.

---

## Why this matters more than it looks

Four research runs have now failed to find a single traceable number for how long any stage of US frontline
retail hiring takes. Not application time, not screening, not scheduling, not the background check, not
onboarding. The fourth run went specifically after the questions the Target conversation was meant to close.

That is not a gap in the searching. It is a property of the market: nobody publishes it, because no retailer
gains anything by publishing it and every vendor gains by quoting an unsourced version of it.

So there are exactly two ways to get these facts. Ask somebody who has them, or build a product that measures
them. Those are not alternatives, they are a sequence, and the second one is already a design requirement.
What follows is about the first one.

---

## What went wrong with the Target route

Worth naming, because it predicts which routes work.

Nishant asked colleagues at Target. It did not happen. The most likely reason is not reluctance to help:
**a current employee discussing internal hiring process with an outside vendor is a confidentiality problem
for them, and a favour that costs them something.** Even a willing person has a reason to be careful.

Every route below is graded partly on whether it asks somebody to take that risk.

## The options

### 0. Anuj Jain's client contacts. NEW 25 Aug 2026, and it goes first

**What it is.** Anuj Jain, product lead at NuPlay, was in the USA a couple of months ago meeting clients and
internal team members. His retail frontline PRD came out of those meetings. He sits next to Nishant, and
Nishant offered to carry questions.

**Why it goes above everything else on this list.** Every other route on this page costs money, weeks, or both,
and starts from a cold contact. This one is a colleague, reachable today, who has already had the conversations
we are trying to arrange. Nobody has asked him for the names.

**Why it was missed.** His PRD was read as a document rather than as a trace of fieldwork. The competitor
teardowns in his repo carry 229 URLs. The PRD documents carry zero. That absence was read as a sourcing
weakness, which it is, but it also means the material behind the PRD came from somewhere other than the web,
and the only candidate is those meetings.

**What to ask.** Four things: who he met, by role and seniority and whether they touch hourly hiring; what kind
of retailer, by size and format and whether any clear the $2B revenue line; what they actually said in their own
words rather than his conclusion from it; and whether notes, a deck, a recording or a Slack thread exist.

**Cost.** One message. Possibly one call.

**What it returns, realistically.** Probably not a warm introduction, because a vendor's client contacts are
guarded and an introduction has a commercial cost to whoever owns the account. What it very likely returns is
role titles and company types, which upgrades our persona guess from three competing options to a shortlist,
and tells us which of the two candidate personas his framing actually came from.

**The risk to name.** A months-old recollection of a client meeting with no notes is a weak source, and it will
be tempting to treat it as strong because it is the only one we have. Per D-003 it stays assumption tier. What
it is genuinely good for is producing names.

**Filed as Q-036.** Also worth folding in: Q-038, whether his design reaches close or stops at offer
acceptance.

### 1. Former employees rather than current ones

**The one route that has not been tried, and the reason it is first.**

A former field HR manager, store manager or recruiter has no confidentiality exposure worth the name on
process questions, no employer approval to seek, and often an appetite to talk about what was broken. The
questions in ../shared/QUESTIONS-FOR-TARGET.md work unchanged.

**How.** LinkedIn search on past company and past title. The titles that matter are in Q-027: Market Human
Resources Manager, Field HR Business Partner, District HR Manager, Area HR Manager, plus store manager and
recruiter. Filter to people who left in the last two or three years, so the process they describe is still
the current one.

**Cost.** Time, and a message that does not read as a sales approach. Realistically fifty messages for three
or four conversations.

**What it returns.** The handoff count, the system-versus-email split, the rehire control, where candidates
disappear. Everything the Target conversation was for.

**The weakness.** Their information has a date on it. Somebody who left in 2023 describes a 2023 process, and
Workday configurations change. Ask when they left and treat the answer as of that date.

### 2. Candidate-side evidence, at scale and for free

**Not interviews. Public accounts of the same funnel from the other end.**

Applicants post dated accounts of their own hiring: applied on this date, heard back after this long,
orientation was on a particular day of the week, first shift was this far out. Interview-review sections on
the large job boards carry thousands of these per employer, and they are written by the person the funnel
happened to.

**What it returns.** The one number four research runs could not find: elapsed time from application to first
shift, with a spread rather than an average, because you get many accounts rather than one. Also the weekly
cadences, which is where "chose not to compress" hides. An orientation held every Monday is a five-day wait
for anybody who clears on a Tuesday, and that is a fact about the process rather than an opinion about it.

**Cost.** A few hours of reading. No permission needed from anybody.

**The weakness, and it is real.** Self-selected and self-reported. People post when the experience was
memorable, which skews to the extremes, and nobody checks their dates. This is `[ASSUMPTION]` tier and must
be labelled that way wherever it lands. It is directional evidence where the project currently has none,
which is worth something, but it is not a measurement and should never be presented as one.

### 3. A paid research panel

**The fastest reliable route, and the only one that can be scheduled.**

Panel services recruit to a screener and pay participants. A screener of "currently responsible for hiring
hourly staff at a US retailer with more than fifty locations" is the kind of thing they exist to fill.

**Cost.** Money, per participant, plus a platform fee. It needs somebody's approval, which makes it a
decision rather than an action.

**Why it is worth pricing anyway.** Against four research runs that returned nothing on the timing questions,
a handful of paid conversations is cheap. The number is worth putting in front of whoever approves spend,
even if the answer is no.

**The weakness.** Panel participants are self-selected in a different way: people who sign up to be
interviewed for money. Volume fixes some of that and nothing fixes all of it.

### 4. Nurix's own network

**Probably thin, but it costs one question to find out.**

Unifi is aviation ground services, so the existing customer relationship is the wrong vertical. The question
is whether anybody in Nurix sales or customer success has a US retail contact from a previous role or a
pipeline conversation that did not close.

**Cost.** One message. Do it regardless of what else is chosen, because the downside is nothing.

### 5. Industry bodies and events

Retail trade associations run HR-focused sessions, and their attendee lists are the exact population. Slow,
seasonal, and gated on a conference calendar, so it is a route for the next quarter rather than this week.

## What to do if the answer is none of them

**Going without is a legitimate choice, as long as it is a choice.** It has three consequences and they
should be written down rather than discovered later.

1. **No success metric can have a real baseline.** Every claim about improvement becomes a claim about the
   product's own instrumentation, measured from the day it switches on. That is defensible, but it means the
   first customer conversation cannot open with a number.
2. **The first deployment carries the measurement burden**, which is a design requirement on v1 rather than a
   research task. It is already recorded that way.
3. **The tier 1 ranking rests on inference rather than observation.** The steps proposed as first are the ones
   nobody sells into and nobody owns, which is a sound argument from the funnel map. It is not the same as
   somebody saying that is where their week goes.

None of those is fatal. All three are worth saying out loud to Nishant rather than letting him find them.
