# 04-segmentation: who we sell to, and which humans decide

Phase 4. Two separate questions here, and they get muddled constantly.

## The company question

Which retailers? Define it by things you can actually look up: retail segment, number of stores,
roughly how many people they hire a year, and what systems they already run. "Mid-market retail" is not
a definition. "US specialty retail, 200 to 900 stores, hiring 5,000+ a year, running Workday" is.

This is usually called the ICP, meaning ideal customer profile.

## The people question

Companies do not buy things, people do. For a named account, work out who is who:

- The user. Whoever touches it daily.
- The champion. Whoever wants it and will argue for it internally.
- The economic buyer. Whoever controls the money and signs.
- The technical evaluator. Whoever checks it against security and IT rules.
- The blocker. Whoever can stop it, often for reasons that have nothing to do with the product.

## The complication specific to this market

Budget often sits with store operations, not just HR. Central recruiting may sign the contract, but the
store manager is usually the one who has to actually use it. If store operations doesn't get enough out
of it to bother, nobody makes a fuss, the tool quietly goes unused, and the renewal dies without anyone
having a meeting about it. Design for that.

## The gate

Do you know who signs, who uses it, and who can kill it, by job title, at a company you can name?
