## Two numbers not to say

**Do not say Paradox does 72 hours.** There is no source for it anywhere. Their published average is three and
a half days and the Chipotle case is four days, confirmed twice as application to first day worked. If it comes
up, the useful version is that the 72 hours is most likely somebody back-computing an inconsistent 75% headline,
because a literal 75% cut from twelve days is exactly three days.

**Do not say 19% of hires are returning workers.** That came from a payroll vendor's blog and it is withdrawn.
The number is **8.9% of US retail hires in 2024**, from Census Quarterly Workforce Indicators, which is
government payroll data and cannot be attacked. Say two things before he does: it is a lower bound because it
only sees returns within four quarters, and retail sits below the all-private figure of 12.7% with no
fourth-quarter spike, so it does not support a seasonal argument.

## If he pushes on speed

Our target is five to six days and it is deliberate headroom, recorded as D-027.

**Against the industry baseline it is strong.** Forty to forty-two days from iCIMS, high thirties to low forties
from SHRM, and forty is the figure you used yourself. Five to six days against forty is an 85% reduction.

**Against Paradox it is slower and there is a good answer.** The fixed clocks are identical for everybody. A
criminal-only background check takes twenty-four to forty-eight hours whoever orders it, and adding employment
verification adds one to three days on top, which is why hourly packages are criminal-only. So the whole
competition sits in the compressible remainder, and that is where our funnel map says seventeen of nineteen
classifiable steps are waits or handoffs. **Speed is the engineering goal. It is not the pitch.**

The pitch is the connector position from D-020, that the retailer keeps what they already run, and the spanning
view from D-013 across all twenty steps including the ones we never automate.

## If he asks what is still open

**Pricing**, deliberately, with two guardrails: the demo shows no price and frames nothing per hire, because
the best study of large US retailers finds gross hiring volume falls when retention improves, so a per-hire
price falls exactly when the product works. There are now three models on the table rather than one, including
charging nothing for the software and making the margin on employee insurance.

**Whether job board distribution is in or out**, which is Q-041. The recommendation is to take the free tier
because nobody can refuse it, open the Indeed conversation because it is slow and free, and buy breadth from
JobTarget only if a customer asks for a destination count.

**Whether contingent staffing is in scope**, which Joy asked about twice. Q-034, and the answer is probably no
for v1.

**Whether we store social security numbers ourselves**, D-029. It is the right key for matching a former
employee and it cannot be the key at application, because it does not exist yet. Storing them makes the
security review much heavier, so the question worth asking the engineers is whether we can pass it through to
the embedded I-9 vendor and hold only a token.
