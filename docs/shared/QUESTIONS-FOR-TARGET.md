# Questions for somebody who works in hiring at Target

**For Nishant. Prepared 18 Aug 2026. Ask in order. The first five are the ones that matter.**

---

## The five

**1.** Walk me through the last hourly person you hired. How many different teams touched that one hire between
the application arriving and them working their first shift?

- Who were they?
- Does anybody own the whole run end to end?
- If not, who notices when somebody goes quiet in the middle?

**2.** In calendar days, how long from application submitted to first paid shift? And what is the spread, not the
average?

**3.** Split that time into three buckets and tell me which is biggest.

- Time you cannot compress: background check turnaround, right to work verification, drug screen, mandated
  training.
- Time you have chosen not to compress: batching, weekly interview slots, approval queues.
- Time that is pure queueing, where nobody is doing anything.

**4.** Which parts of hiring run inside a system, and which parts are somebody sending an email or picking up the
phone? Step by step: application, screening, booking the interview, the offer, the background check, the I-9, the
first shift. For each one, is it Workday, another tool, or a person emailing somebody?

**5.** Somebody was terminated for cause at one store and applies to a different store six months later. What
stops them being hired again? Is that control a system, a shared list, or somebody remembering? Has it ever
failed?

---

## Cost

**6.** Does anybody put a number on what hiring operations cost per year? Is there a cost-per-hire figure that
gets reported internally? What does it include: recruiter time, advertising, screening vendors, referral bonuses,
the store manager's hours?

**7.** When you over-hire for a season, what does that cost? Has anybody measured people being idle, or does it
just get absorbed? Who would see it?

**8.** What share of hiring is done by a third party rather than in house? For seasonal peaks specifically? And
do those firms hand over a pool of people already screened and shortlisted, or just applications?

## Systems

**9.** What does Workday actually do in hiring, as opposed to everything else it does? What does a recruiter do
inside Workday in a normal week, and what do they have to do outside it because Workday does not cover it?

**10.** Where do applications physically live? One central repository, per region, or per store? Does a store see
only its own? If a store wanted a list of everybody who applied to them last quarter, could they get it, and who
would they ask?

**11.** Which vendors are involved in the background check and the I-9? Do their results flow into Workday
automatically, or does somebody retype them?

## Losses

**12.** What share of accepted offers never work a first shift? What share work fewer than five shifts?

**13.** Where do candidates disappear, and how much of that happens after you have already decided you want
them?

**14.** What happens when a new hire goes quiet between the offer and their start date? Is there a report, a
reminder, or does somebody notice on the Monday?

## People and incentives

**15.** Who owns time-to-hire, and what happens to that person if it doubles? Who owns 90-day retention, and
what happens if it halves?

**16.** Does a store manager see a hiring cost or a hiring budget at all, or is it absorbed centrally?

**17.** How many stores does one field HR person cover?

## Needs data access

**18.** Do faster-hired employees stay longer, shorter, or the same, holding store and season constant?

**19.** What is your turnover rate including people with under a year of service?

**20.** Did anything in the hiring process change at the same time as your last wage or scheduling change?
