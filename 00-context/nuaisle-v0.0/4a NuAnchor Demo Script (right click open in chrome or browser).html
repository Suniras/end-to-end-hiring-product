1 0:0012 sec /demo/live

The hook — why this decision matters

Say

“Every season, a brand commits most of its cash to what it's going to buy — months before anything sells. Get it wrong and you're either sold out of the good stuff or discounting the rest. NuAnchor makes that call with you. Let's do a real one, start to finish.”

Do

Nothing yet. Let the empty screen sit for a beat while you say the line — it makes the fill-in later feel like something happened.

Feed it your data — prove it isn't a video

Do
  1. Click ⬇ Download the sample CSV first. Open it — let them see it is an ordinary spreadsheet of styles and sell-through.
  2. Drag that file back onto the drop zone. (Short on time? Use the sample file does it in one click.)
  3. Pause on the confirmation line: 8 styles matched.
Say

“This is a sell-through file — the kind of export any planner can pull in a minute. I'll drag it in. Nothing here is pre-recorded: everything you're about to see is calculated from this file, and on a real call this would be yours.”

Why this scene earns its 30 seconds It kills the biggest silent objection in an AI demo — “is this a canned video?” Showing the file, then dragging it in, answers it before anyone asks.

Stress it live — ask the awkward question yourself

Do
  1. Click Demand runs +15%. The whole plan re-derives in one pass — watch all four tiles move together.
  2. Click it again to switch it off, then click Tariff shock: Vietnam +12pts.
  3. Leave the levers off before you move on, so the PO matches what you described in scene 4.
Say

“Your finance team is going to ask ‘what if it sells faster than that?' — so let's ask now. One click. Demand up 15%, and the entire plan re-derives — the buy, the cost, the supplier quantities, all at once. No second spreadsheet, no analyst rebuilding it overnight.”

“And the one everybody's living through: a duty jump in Vietnam. Same units, but the bill goes up $25,000 — and because it had already spread the order, the risk score doesn't move. That's what the hedge was for.”

This is the scene that wins skeptics Most planning tools can show you a number. Very few let a stranger change an assumption mid-meeting and have every downstream number move in one pass. If the room only remembers one scene, make it this one.

Explaining it to someone with no retail background

New to the team? Start here. No jargon, and it's the version to use on a first call.

Say

Imagine you run a clothing brand.

In March you have to decide how many winter jackets to order — before a single customer has tried one on. Order too many and you're discounting them in January. Order too few and you're sold out in November, watching people leave.

Most brands make that call in a spreadsheet, using a price a factory emailed them.

Then import taxes started jumping. So now even the price isn't fixed — a jacket you budgeted at $40 can cost you $45 by the time it clears customs. That difference is your profit.

NuAnchor does that whole calculation. How many to order, which factory to use, and what it will really cost after taxes — and it shows its working, so a person still makes the final call.

When the client saysThey mean
Sell-throughhow fast something sold. "59% sell-through" = they've sold 59 of every 100 they bought.
Landed costthe real price per unit once shipping and import taxes are added. The number that decides profit.
The buythe whole order for a season. "The season buy" is the single biggest cheque they write.
Markdowna discount they didn't want to give, because they bought too many.
Stockoutsold out of something people still wanted. Lost sales, invisible on any report.
MOQminimum order quantity — the factory won't make fewer than X, so small orders don't work.
OTB / open-to-buyhow much money they have left to spend on stock this season.
Purchase order (PO)the actual order document sent to the factory. NuAnchor's output.
The one-sentence version A brand has to bet most of a season's money on what will sell, months early, and import taxes keep moving the price. NuAnchor makes that call with them and shows its reasoning. If you can say that, you can open a conversation.

Words

Say the left column. The right column loses rooms.

Say thisNot thisWhy
“It decides how much to buy”“agentic decision chain”Buyers don't buy architecture. They buy the decision.
“It shows its work”“glass-box explainability”Everyone understands showing your work. Nobody asked for a glass box.
“A person says yes”“human-in-the-loop approval gate”The plain version is the reassuring one.
“What it'll cost landed, duties included”“landed-cost & tariff optimisation layer”Say the money, not the module.
“It gives you a range, not one fake-precise number”“90% confidence interval”Same fact, no statistics lesson.
“Overnight it cleared the routine ones”“autonomous exception triage”Describe the morning, not the mechanism.
Two words to keep “Landed cost” and “sell-through” are the client's own words, not ours — a merch planner uses both daily. Using them correctly is how you sound like you've done this before.

The four questions you will get

Is this running on real data?

No — and say so plainly. The sample season is synthetic; the app says so at the bottom of the screen. What's real is the machinery: the same engines produce every number you see, and it runs on a file you hand it. Then make it an ask: “send us a sell-through export and the next demo is yours.”

Can I trust AI to commit my inventory?

Point at the gate. The agent cannot place an order — approval is the only exit, the limits are set by the client (supplier policy, budget envelope, minimum order quantities), and every decision is logged with its reasoning. If they're still cold, offer to run it in parallel with their existing process for one season and compare.

How accurate is the forecast?

Don't sell accuracy you can only prove a season later. Sell what's checkable today: the reasoning holds together, and the landed-cost and duty maths can be verified against their own invoices line by line. Then mention the loop — after the season the read is scored against what actually sold, so next season starts sharper.

We already have a planning tool. / Doesn't Blue Yonder do this?

Two different answers. Their existing tool: this sits on top and does the handoffs it doesn't — nobody's ripping anything out. The enterprise suites: they're priced and staffed for a Fortune 500 and take a year or more to stand up; a $200M brand can't buy that, and this is running today. Slides 4 and 5 of the launch deck have the detail — use them, don't improvise the competitive answer.

If it goes wrong on camera

The run stalls

Use the skip

⇥ Skip to the staged PO jumps to the finished order. Keep narrating — the PO is what matters, not the animation.

Numbers look odd

A lever is still on

Check step 3 — a lever left switched on changes everything downstream. Click it off, or Reset the demo and start the scene again.

Someone asks something you can't answer

Write it down out loud

“Good question, I don't want to guess — let me come back to you today.” Then post it in #nustack. Every one of those is a roadmap input.

Fall back to the recording
