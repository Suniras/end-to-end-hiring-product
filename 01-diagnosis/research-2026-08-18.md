## Size of the prize

Start with the plain answer. **No large US retailer discloses what it spends on hiring.** There is no number to quote, and anybody who hands you one has built it.

What the search actually shows, stated precisely:

- In EDGAR full text search, `"cost per hire"` restricted to 10-K forms returns 54 matching *documents* across all filers. That is documents, not occurrences. The top hits are Onity Group (the renamed Ocwen Financial, a mortgage servicer) and US Steel. Not retailers.
- `"talent acquisition expense"` in 10-Ks returns zero.
- All three cost phrases return zero at all six retailer CIKs tested. So do nine variants: recruitment costs, hiring costs, cost to hire, recruitment expense, recruiting expenses, recruitment expenses, costs per hire, hiring expenses, cost of turnover.
- Say "2001 forward", not "ever". EDGAR full text search only indexes from 2001.

Turnover is the same story. Walmart has discussed associate turnover in proxies repeatedly, including in management's own compensation discussion in the 2020 proxy ("resulting in lower associate turnover"), and has never attached a figure to it. Target's FY2021 10-K says many team members are in positions with "historically high turnover rates", with no figure. Costco's 2017 proxy claims "the lowest employee turnover rates" in the industry, with no figure. The pattern is qualitative discussion, never a number.

### The inputs, each labelled

**Disclosed, Walmart FY2026 10-K, accession 0000104169-26-000055, filed 2026-03-13:**
- approximately 1.6 million US associates, 2.1 million worldwide
- approximately 92% of US associates hourly, approximately 68% full-time
- total revenues $713.2 billion (income statement line 713,163)
- operating, selling, general and administrative expenses $147.943 billion (FY2025: $139.884 billion), confirmed against the XBRL API
- the word "turnover" appears exactly once in the whole filing, and it refers to inventory

**Disclosed, BLS:**
- Retail trade annual hires 2025: 6,926,000. Series JTU440000000000000HIL, period M13, which is BLS's own annual total. 2024 was 7,360,000.
- Retail trade employment 2025: 15,441,100. Series CEU4200000001, M13.
- Retail trade hires rate 2025: 45.0. Series JTU440000000000000HIR, monthly values summed. 2024 was 47.6.
- Fetchable source: `download.bls.gov/pub/time.series/jt/jt.data.3.Hires`. Do not cite `api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2/timeseries/data/`. That is POST only and returns 405 on a click.

**Survey, not disclosed, not academic. SHRM 2022 benchmarking report, data collected April to November 2021 from a random sample of SHRM members, no response rate published, no peer review:**
- Cost per hire: n=472, 25th $354, median $1,244, 75th $4,375, average $4,683
- HR expense per FTE: n=436, 25th $435, median $1,093, 75th $2,925, average $5,677
- HR expense as share of operating expense: n=298, 25th 0.4%, median 1.4%, 75th 5.3%, average 7.2%
- HR staff to employees: n=945, 25th 0.42, median 1.11, 75th 2.02, average 1.70
- SHRM's own instruction: "It is recommended that the median be used as a basis for all interpretations of the data when the average and median are discrepant." Here average over median is 4683/1244 = 3.8, so use the median.

### Constructed, with the arithmetic shown

Hire volume:

```
45.0 hires per 100 employees        [disclosed, BLS retail trade 2025 hires rate]
x 1,600,000 US associates           [disclosed, Walmart FY2026 10-K]
= 720,000 hire events per year      [constructed]
```

The independent check: 6,926,000 / 15,441,100 = 44.86%, which gives 718,400. The constructed ratio and BLS's published rate agree to a tenth of a point. Use the published rate so you are not defending your own arithmetic.

Two assumptions inside that number, both mine, neither disclosed. First, that Walmart hires at the retail sector average. Nobody has published Walmart's rate, so this could be wrong in either direction. Second, JOLTS hires are events, not people. One person hired, separated and rehired is three events. So 720,000 is hire events, not 720,000 individuals.

Cost band:

```
720,000 x $354   = $255 million   [25th percentile, SHRM member survey]
720,000 x $1,244 = $896 million   [median, SHRM member survey]
720,000 x $4,375 = $3.15 billion  [75th percentile, SHRM member survey]
```

The defensible band is $255 million to $3.15 billion, and the median case is roughly $900 million. Do not quote $3.4 billion. That comes from the $4,683 average, which is a mean and not a percentile, so it does not belong on the end of a percentile range.

Scale check, my arithmetic: $896 million is 0.61% of Walmart's FY2026 SG&A and 0.13% of revenue.

Honest caveat on the band. It varies only cost per hire and holds volume fixed. The volume term is probably the larger source of error, and it is not bounded. So the range is wider than $255 million to $3.15 billion, not narrower.

### Total HR spend, for context

Two SHRM ratios both land near $1.5 to $2 billion for all of Walmart HR. They are not independent. Same report, same sample, same collection window, overlapping respondents.

```
$1,093 HR expense per FTE  x  1,600,000  = $1.75 billion   [wrong: per-FTE times headcount]
1.4% of $147.943 billion SG&A            = $2.07 billion   [global SG&A, 2.1M associates]
```

The first line is inflated because SHRM's metric is explicitly per FTE and Walmart's 1.6 million is headcount, of which only 68% is full-time. If part-timers count as half an FTE, which is my assumption and not a disclosure:

```
1,600,000 x 0.68              = 1,088,000 full-time
1,600,000 x 0.32 x 0.5        =   256,000 part-time FTE  [0.5 is my assumption]
                                ---------
                                1,344,000 FTE
x $1,093                      = $1.47 billion             [constructed]
```

The second line is a global number covering 2.1 million associates, so it is not a second estimate of the same quantity. Per head the two are consistent, $1,093 per US associate against $986 per global associate, which is worth saying. The apparent convergence at "$2 billion" is partly an artefact of comparing a US figure to a global one.

Table 4 checks to the cent: search costs CHF 4,391.06 against hiring costs CHF 20,744.29, n=3,947. It is the best sourced item in the whole set. ### One lead worth chasing

Amazon publishes its US seasonal hiring figure in its own newsroom. Reportedly 250,000 for the 2025 holiday season with "over $1 billion into additional pay and benefits". ## How many teams touch one hire

### What is establishable

The organisational shape is establishable from live job postings. The handoff count is not.

**Walmart, Market People Partner.** Req R-2573728, live, hosted at "(USA) NY WILLIAMSVILLE 02355 WM SUPERCENTER", posted 2026-07-20, band $90,000 to $180,000. The posting says the role "Manages the talent acquisition strategy for assigned stores by developing, communicating, and delivering recruiting strategies", and inside that same bullet, "coordinating with hiring managers to arrange interviews" and "gathering and facilitating feedback from managers on candidates".

Read that carefully. Everything after "by" is Walmart's standard gerund chain describing how a strategy is managed. It is market-level ownership of recruiting strategy sitting in a field role physically hosted in a supercenter. The bullet implies two transfers, out to the store hiring manager for interviews and back for feedback. Not four. And nothing in that bullet scopes it to hourly. The only hourly reference on the page is a different bullet: "Leads the execution of hourly and salaried Workforce Management programs".

**Walmart, corporate talent acquisition.** Req R-2592770, Bentonville home office. The team blurb says it supports "over 20,000 home office associates ... across Retail, Corporate Functions, Growth, and Technology." That is a floor, not a point, and the scope list includes the word "Retail", so it cannot be cleanly read as home office only. It is recruitment marketing copy inside a job ad, which is the weakest grade of self-report.

**Walmart, distribution centre HR.** Req R-2518099, Laurens SC, $84,000 to $126,000, says in its own words "Lead the People Team at the distribution center", and describes partnering with central talent acquisition on sourcing, screening and offers. This is the single cleanest artifact in the set. It also proves central TA is not confined to the home office. A separate req, R-2575199 at an eCommerce warehouse in Chino CA, puts benefits, hiring, transfers, terminations, payroll and new hire orientation in one job description, plus new hire orientation. That is a co-location of duties in one role. It is not a measured handoff count.

**Kroger, District Human Resources Specialist.** Req 220011, pulled live from the Oracle tenant, posted 2026-08-18. It names three organisational counterparties: talent acquisition, the division HR department, and store leadership. On requisitions it says the role "Support[s] talent acquisition in the creation and ongoing maintenance of hiring requisitions", which reads as requisition creation sitting with TA, though that is a reading and not a statement. The same role holds both decision rights and data entry: "Conduct interviews, make hiring decisions", "Lead and support store leadership", and "Process data entries related to wages/salaries, terminations, status and position changes, newly hired and rehired associates, transfers".

Warning. That requisition's posted end date is 2026-08-26. It is the load-bearing Kroger citation and it expires within days of this being written. Screenshot it or replace it.

**Target, store Human Resources Expert.** The evergreen page says "Work with your HR leader to anticipate and address specific talent and staffing needs". Work with, not report to. The reporting line is not in the source.

**Target, corporate HR.** Five sub-teams, not six: Compensation & Benefits, Human Resources Generalist, Learning & Development, Talent Acquisition, Talent Management. The URL is `corporate.target.com/careers/career-areas/corporate/human-resources`. The obvious guess without `/corporate/` returns 404. The Talent Acquisition description in full is: "Attract talent and act as a strategic advisor for company-wide hiring initiatives. Provide an exceptional job candidate experience from the first conversation through interviews, hiring and beyond." Both sentences matter. Target corporate TA is described as being on the candidate path from first conversation through hiring, so it cannot be characterised as pure strategy work removed from filling roles.

**Systems.** Walmart and Target both run Workday. Walmart on `wd504.myworkdayjobs.com`, Target hourly store applications resolving to `target.wd5.myworkdayjobs.com/targetcareers/`. Kroger runs Oracle. So two of the three biggest names share an ATS vendor. Walmart separately posts SAP payroll systems engineering roles (R-2357368), which proves an SAP payroll estate exists but says nothing about where the org boundary sits.

### What is not establishable

- **The handoff count per hire.** No retailer publishes one. Every count in circulation is somebody's tally of bullet points in a job ad.
- **Timing at a retailer.** No retailer discloses time to fill for hourly. SHRM's nonexecutive median of 44 days (n=840) is a cross-industry member survey, not retail and not observed.
- **Reporting lines.** Job ads describe who you work with, not who signs your review.
- **Whether DC hiring is simpler than store hiring.** This was asserted and then contradicted by Walmart's own Laurens SC posting, where site HR partners with central TA. Drop it.

### The only measured multi-party sequence anyone publishes

It is federal, not retail. OPM's hiring roadmap, stamped March 2017, and the companion Hiring Elements document. Independently recomputed:

- 14 hiring elements
- exactly three distinct owners: Human Resources Office (7 elements), Manager (3), Human Resources Office plus Security Office (2), Manager plus Human Resources Office (2)
- 13 elements carry day allotments: 1, 1, 1, 5, 2, 10, none, 15, 1, 15, 3, 10, 2, 14. They sum to exactly 80.
- ownership changes hands five times, at elements 3, 5, 10, 11 and 13
- the manager's largest block is 15 days, which is 18.75% of 80. Do not call it the longest step. "Evaluate Applications" is also 15 days, owned by the HR Office. It is tied.
- OPM names the problem itself in a "Hiring Contract" and diagnoses the two-way complaint on page 5

Useful as a shape and as an order of magnitude for how many owners and how many changes of hands a formal hiring process contains. Not transferable to Target as a number.

### On manager discretion

Hoffman, Kahn and Li, NBER working paper, "Discretion in Hiring". Sample is 15 firms employing low skilled service sector workers, nearly 3,700 applicant pools averaging 260 applicants, nearly 266,000 hires. The paper explicitly says the job "is not a retail store job".

The correct statement of the headline: across those pools, **on average 22% of hires are exceptions to the test recommendation**, meaning a lower scoring applicant was hired while a higher scoring one who applied was not. It is not the share of pools where an override happened. The denominator is total hires.

On tenure, the paper reports passed over greens and yellows staying "roughly 14% and 12% longer, respectively" for the exception-hired comparison, and separately a noisier estimate that "passed over greens stay 4% longer than yellows hired before them from the same pool." Quote both or neither. And these are 2015 working paper numbers. Do not attribute them to the 2018 QJE article without checking they survived peer review.

## Outsourcing, and the third-party pool idea

### The share, which is knowable and small

Retail barely uses staffing agencies. The primary source is the BLS Contingent Work Supplement, July 2023, release USDL-24-2267, published 8 November 2024, Table 6. Temp workers are classified by the industry they are assigned to, not the agency's industry.

| Assignment industry, 2023 | Temp agency workers | Share of all temp agency workers |
|---|---|---|
| All temporary help agency workers | 945,000 | 100% |
| Retail trade | 19,000 | 2.0% |
| Manufacturing | 123,000 | 13.0% |
| Professional and business services | 269,000 | 28.5% |

Retail trade employment in that release is 16,673,000, so agency temps are 0.11% of retail employment. The direction has been down for decades. A Census working paper (CES 23-64, Table A.2) gives retail's share of temp assignments as 5.30% in 1995 falling to 2.90% in 2017.

For scale on the sector as a whole:
- Employment services (NAICS 5613) revenue, Census QSS release CB26-99, 11 June 2026, Table 1a, seasonally adjusted: 1Q26 $109,468M, 4Q25 $106,967M, 3Q25 $109,579M, 2Q25 $107,564M, 1Q25 $108,944M. The four 2025 quarters sum to $433,054M. But 5613 includes 561330 Professional Employer Organizations, whose revenue includes payroll they administer for clients. So $433 billion is not the size of the staffing and recruiting market. Do not use it that way.
- Employment services employment, July 2026, preliminary: 3,206,800. Temp help alone: 2,505,000.

And no retailer says it uses an RPO. `"recruitment process outsourcing"` in EDGAR across all form types since 2020 returns 629 hits across 38 unique filers. Every SIC code was checked. **Zero filers in retail SIC 52xx through 59xx.** Chewy is the closest thing to an exception, and its FY2025 10-K names staffing agencies in one clause of the Human Capital section with no volume and no spend.

The RPO vendors say the same thing from their side. Korn Ferry's RPO fee revenue was $367.1 million in FY2026, 12.6% of $2.907 billion total, supporting "more than 250 enterprise clients". TrueBlue's PeopleSolutions segment was $187.7 million, 11.6% of $1.616 billion, and the filing says "Revenue for our PeopleScout business declined" while an acquisition "contributed 35.4% of growth". So organic RPO shrank. ManpooerGroup notes "decreased activity in our RPO permanent recruitment business".

One important trap. Korn Ferry's 10-K contains the word "retail" zero times, but that is a taxonomy artefact, not evidence. Korn Ferry reports revenue by industry in six buckets, and retail is not one of them. Retailers land in Consumer Goods, which is $348.4 million, 12.0% of fee revenue. Do not run the "zero mentions" argument.

### The pool idea, straight

The narrow version survives. **No vendor sells a pre-screened, shortlisted candidate pool that a customer's system can draw from programmatically as a product.** Nothing found in this research contradicts that.

The broad version does not survive. "The gap is real and unfilled" is too strong, because programmatic, stage-filtered, background-check-aware candidate retrieval already exists in this exact market segment.

What is actually there, by mechanism:

| Thing | Direction of data flow | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| Fountain, `developer.fountain.com`, v2 REST, docs updated 9 Jan 2026 | You pull | `List All Applicants` filtered by funnel, stage, and stage type (hired, rejected, archived, background_check, interview, signature), plus labels and created or last-transitioned dates. Docs say you can retrieve "all of the information about an applicant, including their scorecard results, background check information, and labels." Also `Advance an Applicant`, bulk transition history, and Hire webhooks. |
| Indeed partner docs | They push to you | "Direct employer integration with Indeed Apply", a Candidates section, and a Disposition Sync API. The docs describe specifying a URL per job, with Indeed sending candidate information as JSON, 60 day retention, automatic retries. Cloudflare gated, so treat as a strong lead not a citation. |
| SmartRecruiters | Push into your ATS | Post an Application, Get Application Screening Questions and Privacy Policies, Get Candidate Application Status. |
| Talroo | Outbound only from you | Applications arrive by email and dashboard download only. The only candidate-related API is the Disposition API v1.0.0, `POST /disposition/v1/events`, with an enum including screening, hired and application_qualified. Docs copyright 2024. |
| ZipRecruiter resume database | Worked in your ATS | The closest thing to a pool. No publicly readable API docs. `ziprecruiter.com/partner/documentation` exists and returns 403 without credentials, so it is gated, not absent. |
| Instawork | App only | Vetting is substantive, "up to eight different methods", article dated 1 May 2025. No developer docs exist; developer and docs subdomains fail DNS. Instawork's own positioning names retail. |
| HR Open Standards, SearchDocument | Schema, not a service | A published open standard. What the page actually offers to download is HR-XML Recruiting 3.3 and 3.2. |

Two conclusions.

First, Fountain is the answer to "is there anything real to plug into". It is a high volume hourly hiring platform rather than a third party labour pool, so it is your customer's own applicant store, not a shared market. But the shape you wanted, "give me every applicant in this funnel who cleared background check and is at interview stage", is already a documented public API in hourly hiring. That reframes the opportunity from "build the missing capability" to "the capability exists inside single tenants, and nobody has pooled it across employers".

Second, notice the direction of travel in that table. Almost everything published moves status outward from the employer, or pushes candidates into an ATS. Very little lets an outside system pull candidates in. That asymmetry is the actual structural fact, and it is more defensible than "the gap is unfilled".

One boundary to respect. The 2.0% retail figure is about temp help agency assignments. Gig staffing platforms sit outside that CWS definition. Do not use the CWS number to argue that retail does not use outsourced hourly labour of any kind.

## The compliance vendor question

### Is GreenLight real

Yes, and it is not what it was assumed to be. **GreenLight is a worker classification vendor, not an I-9 or E-Verify vendor.** Its own page says it will "Instantly determine and document W2 vs. 1099 status, powered by up-to-date legal logic." The overall positioning is workforce compliance across onboarding, classification, payments and tax, with legal logic attributed to Littler Mendelson. The site mentions I-9, E-Verify and background checks zero times. If GreenLight came up as a comparable in the E-Verify conversation, it is the wrong comparable.

### The rules the integration has to live inside

Four DHS facts that shape any design.

DHS does not certify employer agents. That page was last updated 07/11/2022. Web services access needs enrolment plus a technical certification process, and the Interface Control Agreement is distributed only inside authenticated environments, so you cannot read the spec before committing. The ICA header still reads "Current as of September, 2019".

There is no case status that says "contesting". There are six case results and none is a countdown. But E-Verify does report a downstream signal: Case in Continuance means "The employee has contacted DHS or visited an SSA field office, but more time is needed". So the contest is written in by the employer and confirmed back indirectly, never surfaced as a "contesting" state.

There are three clocks, not two, all in federal government working days, which DHS defines as "Monday through Friday (except for federal holidays)":
1. Employer must notify the employee and complete the referral "as soon as possible within 10 federal government working days". The employee's decision is due on that same 10th day, so it is a shared window.
2. Employee has 8 federal working days from referral to contact DHS or visit SSA.
3. DHS and SSA have 10 federal government working days from referral to update the result. DHS's own instruction is "Check E-Verify periodically for an update on the case result." That is DHS telling you to poll.

The adverse action bar is broader than termination. That page was last reviewed 03/12/2026. This is the strongest single fact in the angle and it survives unqualified.

### The vendors, and the question that decides it

The decisive question: can an integrator read the E-Verify case state, including that a tentative nonconfirmation is being contested and how many days are left?

| Vendor | Reads case status | Reads a deadline | Reads the contest / TNC action | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WorkBright | Yes, full status enum | Yes, `deadline_on` | Yes, `tnc_action`, plus `fan_url`, `rdc_url`, and both Spanish variants. Also an `/everify/cases/action_required` endpoint. | The only one that publicly documents both. No literal `tentative_nonconfirmation` status value exists in the enum, and some statuses carry a `visit_everify` caveat, meaning read only for those and the action must happen in E-Verify itself. Docs updated June 2025. |
| Symmetry I-9 | Same as WorkBright | Same | Same | It is WorkBright, white labelled. Commercial partnership announced 10 June 2025, not an acquisition. |
| Equifax Guardian | Yes | Yes, `eVerifyNextStepDueDate` | Not documented | Gives you a due date. Explicitly not a guaranteed event stream, so you poll. Which lines up with DHS's own polling instruction. |
| Checkr | Thin. Four `form_i9` order-progress values only | No | No | Full `form_i9` object was pulled. | Mitratech Tracker | Yes, in product | Yes, in product | Yes, in product | No integrator surface established here. **Said plainly: of the vendors verified here, only WorkBright publicly documents both a readable deadline and a readable TNC action. Equifax Guardian gives a due date and nothing about the contest. Everyone else, no. And nobody publishes a push event that tells you an employee is contesting with N days remaining. Every workable design polls.**

Two corrections to expectations you may be carrying in.

The DHS Employer Search does include employer agents. Its own release note says the database "only includes employers, E-Verify employer agents, and federal contractors who have self-reported that their company has five or more employees." The problem is not coverage. The returned fields carry no agent flag, so you can confirm a vendor has an E-Verify account, and whether it opted into E-Verify+, but not whether it is an agent account. Also note DHS contradicts itself on freshness on the same page, saying it "updates the search tool daily at approximately 2 a.m. ET" while elsewhere supporting a staleness read.

The remote Section 2 alternative procedure is more flexible than assumed. Yes, it requires E-Verify enrolment. But DHS expressly says: "you may choose to offer the alternative procedure for remote hires only but continue to apply physical examination procedures to all employees who work onsite or in a hybrid capacity, so long as you do not adopt such a practice for a discriminatory purpose or treat employees differently based on their citizenship, immigration status, or national origin." So it is not locked to a per-site setting. It can be a lawful, consistently applied category rule. What is prohibited is a per-individual eligibility judgement.

### The thing nobody looked at, which may matter more than TNC handling

DHS is running a recurring process through 2026, roughly biweekly, publishing revoked Employment Authorization Documents into a **Status Change Report** inside E-Verify. Employers "must immediately begin reverifying every current employee whose EAD the Status Change Report indicates was revoked", must compare each employee's EAD card number against the revoked document number, and must not accept the revoked document. The report carries revocation date, case number, A-number and revoked document number. The 11 August 2026 update expanded coverage to categories C08, C09, A12 and C19. The August 2026 What's New feed is almost entirely TPS termination notices.

This is a second state machine. It is keyed to reverification of the existing workforce, not to new hires. The trigger arrives as a periodic report rather than a per-case status change. None of the vendor APIs above were checked against it, and WorkBright's E-Verify case object has no field for it. For a large hourly retailer in 2026 this is plausibly a bigger live exposure than TNC handling, because it hits the current workforce in bulk.

Three smaller items sitting on pages already read. ICA v32 adds "Optional Client Company Account Enrollment for Employer Agents: Enables employer agents to automate their client company account enrollment process", which is directly relevant to an agent strategy. ICA documentation is consolidating into a single web based resource with Swagger retained, which partly softens the "spec is permanently behind a wall" framing. And the E-Verify MOU, Responsibilities of the Employer, Article II Section A paragraph 13, is where the obligations sit that any contractual remedy would need to reference. It was never read.

## Numbers not to use

Grouped by where they came from. Reasons are short on purpose so this can be scanned before a meeting.

**Cost and size**
1. **$4,129 cost per hire.** FY2015 data, published 8 August 2016, n=2,048. Eleven years old. Also "no industry breakdown at all" describes the news article, not the underlying report, which was sold with industry cuts.
2. **"Cost per hire appears only 54 times in 10-K history."** 54 is matching documents, not occurrences. And EDGAR full text starts at 2001, so nothing supports "ever".
3. **"$0.25B to $3.4B depending on which percentile you pick."** The $3.4B end comes from an average, which is not a percentile. Use $255M to $3.15B for 25th to 75th.
4. **"Two independent SHRM ratios converge on $2B."** One report, one sample, one collection window, overlapping respondents. Not independent, not corroboration.
5. **$1.75 billion for Walmart HR.** Multiplies a per-FTE figure by headcount when only 68% of associates are full-time. Nearer $1.5 billion on any reasonable conversion.
6. **$1.75B and $2.07B as two estimates of the same thing.** One is 1.6 million US associates. The other is 1.4% of global SG&A covering 2.1 million associates worldwide.
7. **21% as a US retail search-cost share.** Swiss firms, 2009, Swiss francs, upper-secondary vocational hires.
8. **$433 billion as the staffing and recruiting market.** NAICS 5613 includes PEOs, whose revenue includes administered client payroll.
9. **ASA's "about 2% of the US nonfarm workforce."** 2.2 million against 2% implies a 110 million base. Nonfarm is around 160 million, so the real share is about 1.4%.

**HR staffing density**
10. **"US retail HR is 5.6x leaner than the SHRM benchmark."** Counts two SOC codes on one side against SHRM's all-HR-staff ratio on the other. Like for like the figure is 3.2x.
11. **28,190 HR specialists in retail trade**, and the companion figures 2,550, 15,503,420, 11,460 and 3,275,200. None could be verified. BLS returned 403 and a rate limit. Plausibility only: search results put retail trade at 25,950 HR specialists in May 2022.
12. **0.355 per 100 for general merchandise.** The division gives 0.350. And 1.11/0.350 is 3.2x, not 3.1x.
13. **"There is no central recruiting function at a US retailer to displace."** OEWS classifies workers by their establishment's NAICS code, so Bentonville, Minneapolis and Cincinnati HQ HR sit in NAICS 551114, Corporate, Subsidiary and Regional Managing Offices, not in retail trade. The function is excluded from the count by construction. You cannot conclude absence from a query that cannot see it. That 551114 cell was never pulled and is the single most important missing number in the whole set.

**Turnover disclosure**
14. **"Walmart's 10-K says a large number of associates turn over each year."** That line appears in 10-Ks filed 2013 through 2020 and is not in the FY2026 10-K, where "turnover" appears once and means inventory.
15. **"The only mention of associate turnover in Walmart's proxy history is a shareholder proposal."** False. `"associate turnover"` returns 5 Walmart filings, `"employee turnover"` returns 21. The 2020 proxy is management's own compensation discussion text.
16. **"Target has no employee turnover disclosure."** False. `"turnover rates"` plural returns 26 Target filings. The FY2021 10-K says team members are in positions with "historically high turnover rates".
17. **Any absence claim built on a singular quoted phrase.** EDGAR does not stem. Across the six retailers, `"turnover rate"` returns 4 filings and `"turnover rates"` returns 64. Every absence claim needs both forms plus synonyms before it counts as an absence.

**Handoffs**
18. **"Four distinct handoffs inside one bullet point."** Two transfers, out for interviews and back for feedback.
19. **"The Market People Partner owns the entire candidate funnel for hourly."** The verb is "Manages the talent acquisition strategy". Nothing in that bullet scopes it to hourly.
20. **"Roughly 20,000 home office associates."** The source says "over 20,000", which is a floor, and the same sentence includes "Retail" in the scope.
21. **"20,000 corporate hires is a rounding error against Walmart's hourly volume."** No denominator was ever cited for the comparison.
22. **"Kroger district HR sits between four counterparties."** Three named organisational counterparties. The fourth item was HRIS, and a system is not a counterparty.
23. **"The Target HR Expert reports to a store HR leader."** The page says "work with your HR leader".
24. **"Six Target corporate HR sub-teams."** Five.
25. **"Target corporate TA is a different job from filling a store shift."** The dropped half of the sentence says corporate TA provides the candidate experience "from the first conversation through interviews, hiring and beyond".
26. **"The retailers do not share an ATS."** Walmart and Target both run Workday.
27. **"DC hiring has fewer handoffs than store hiring."** No source states a handoff count anywhere, and the Laurens SC posting shows DC site HR partnering with central TA.
28. **Walmart's Global People Center owning onboarding.** Killed. The quote is not at the cited URL. The GPC is a Bentonville building, and the one GPC requisition found is a Director of Advanced Analytics.
29. **"Managers override a central recommendation in 22% of applicant pools."** 22% is the average share of *hires* that are exceptions. And the paper's population explicitly excludes retail store jobs.
30. **"Passed-over candidates would have stayed 12 to 14% longer."** That implies a counterfactual. The paper measures observed tenure of passed-over workers who were later hired, a selected subset. And the same passage reports a competing estimate of 4%.
31. **NBER figures attributed to the 2018 QJE article.** They come from the 2015 working paper.

**Outsourcing**
32. **"The latest CWS year is 2017."** The 2023 CWS was published 8 November 2024.
33. **"Manufacturing takes roughly a third of temp assignments."** That was 2017. In 2023 it is 13.0%.
34. **"ASA is the staffing industry's own statistics body, so retail's absence there is meaningful."** ASA is a trade association, and its five categories are occupational, not client industries, so retail could not appear in that list regardless.
35. **"TrueBlue is the largest US-listed pure-play RPO owner."** Not pure play, staffing is 88% of revenue. Not largest, Korn Ferry's RPO fee revenue alone is $367.1M against TrueBlue's whole $187.7M segment.

37. **"Korn Ferry is a top-two RPO provider."** Unsupported by the filing or anything else.
38. **"Zero retail mentions means Korn Ferry does not sell to retail."** Taxonomy artefact. Retailers sit in Consumer Goods, $348.4 million.
39. **"Zero retail and API counts across ManpowerGroup, Kelly and TrueBlue."** TrueBlue names retail five times. Restate as ManpowerGroup and Kelly zero, TrueBlue names retail as an end market but has zero API mentions.
40. **"375 hits for recruitment process outsourcing across all forms since 2020."** Reproduces at 629 today. Do not quote a hit count without the retrieval date.
41. **"Every filer using the phrase is a staffing or HR company."** False. Servotronics, Retractable Technologies, Credicorp, Quest Resource, Distoken, Lixiang and Youlife all use it.
42. **"ZipRecruiter has no documented API."** The partner docs exist and return 403 without credentials. Say no publicly readable documentation.
43. **"No traceable provider offers programmatic candidate retrieval, so the gap is unfilled."** Fountain's public v2 API does stage-filtered, background-check-aware applicant retrieval today.
44. **"Retail is not where outsourced hourly labour goes"** extended to gig platforms. The CWS measures temp help agency assignments. Instawork and its peers fall outside that definition, and Instawork names retail in its own positioning.

**Compliance**
45. **"The DHS employer search does not include employer agents."** It does. They are simply not labelled as agents in the returned fields.
46. **"Every E-Verify web services change is a six-month rebuild with mandatory acceptance testing."** Release v31.1 gave three months and stated "Acceptance testing is not required for this release." The six months rule that does hold is in Supplemental Guide section 2.0, running from the date DHS notifies you.
47. **"E-Verify never reports that an employee is contesting."** Case in Continuance is E-Verify reporting that the employee contacted DHS or visited SSA.
48. **"There are two clocks."** Three. The missed one is DHS and SSA having 10 federal government working days from referral to update the result.
49. **"Equifax discloses an E-Verify mandate as a risk factor."** The sentence sits in Item 1 Business, under "Summary of Regulations Affecting our Employer Services Business", well before Item 1A begins. The wording is "may impact", listed alongside ACA and state unemployment changes as ordinary regulatory dependencies.
50. **"The incumbent category is not growing at the largest incumbent."** Only Employer Services declined. Verification Services revenue rose 8% in 2025 and Workforce Solutions margin rose to 44.2% from 43.3%.
51. **Attaching the 2% and 12% declines to I-9 revenue.** Those are Employer Services movements. I-9 is one named contributor among unemployment claims, ACA, ERC and onboarding. No I-9 dollar figure is disclosed anywhere.
52. **"Remote I-9 must be a per-site configuration, and a candidate-level toggle creates discrimination exposure."** DHS expressly permits a remote-hire versus onsite distinction inside one site.
53. **"Mitratech's business days."** Mitratech says business days where DHS says federal government working days. Those diverge on every federal holiday. Do not compute a deadline from the vendor's wording.

58. The Target corporate HR path without `/corporate/` returns 404.

60. Target's seasonal fact sheet says "$15 to $24 an hour" is a **starting wage** range. Quoting it without "starting wage" turns a floor range into a pay band.

## What is still only answerable by a practitioner

Hand this list to someone who does hourly hiring at Target. Every item is unanswerable from public sources, and each one changes a number or a design decision.

**On volume and cost**
1. How many open requisitions does one store actually carry for hourly, and are they evergreen? If one requisition fills dozens of positions, then requisitions-per-recruiter benchmarks are meaningless, and so is any recruiter headcount derived from them.
2. What share of hire events are rehires, transfers and seasonal conversions rather than net new people? JOLTS counts events, so the 720,000 figure is inflated by an unknown amount.
3. What does Target actually count inside cost per hire, and does an internal figure exist at all? Specifically: does it include store manager and team lead interview time, field HR time, background check fees, agency fees, job board spend, or none of these?
4. Which budget lines does hiring cost land in? Store payroll, field HR, corporate TA, marketing, or somewhere else. This decides who feels the saving.
5. What did the 2025 holiday ramp cost, on any internal measure? Public sources give roughly 43,000 seasonal store team members and no cost.
6. What is Target's own hourly turnover rate? Public filings say "historically high turnover rates" and nothing more.

**On the process shape**
7. Walk one hourly hire end to end and name every role that touches it. Then say how many times the record changes hands in Workday, and how many of those are a person waiting on another person.
8. How long is each wait, in hours not days? Application to screen, screen to interview invitation, invitation to interview, interview to offer, offer to I-9 complete, I-9 to first shift.
9. Where does the funnel leak worst? Which single step loses the most candidates, and is anyone measuring drop-off per step or only overall conversion.
10. Does the store leader or the field HR partner make the actual hiring decision for hourly, and what can a store leader override? Does the system record the override.
11. What is the internal time-to-fill metric for hourly, measured from which event, and who owns it.

**On compliance operations**
12. Who runs E-Verify day to day? Store HR, a shared service centre, or a vendor. Name the vendor.
13. Who receives a tentative nonconfirmation notice today, who physically hands the Further Action Notice to the employee, and what happens across the 10 federal working days. Does anyone poll for status or does someone check a dashboard.
14. Has there ever been an adverse action mistake during a contested TNC, and what triggered it. This is the failure mode with the broadest legal bar.
15. Is Target enrolled in E-Verify at every site? Is the remote Section 2 alternative procedure in use, and if so, under what written category rule.
16. Who handled the 2026 EAD Status Change Reports? How many current employees were flagged, how was reverification tracked, and how long did it take. This is the exposure nobody has sized.
17. Which I-9 vendor is in place, and does anybody integrate with it programmatically or is it a person in a portal.

**On third parties and plumbing**
18. Does Target use staffing agencies or gig platforms for store hourly at all? In which markets, at what markup, and who owns that contract.
19. Does the ATS write dispositions back to Indeed or Talroo? Is that an API, a scheduled file, or nothing.
20. What does Target's Workday recruiting configuration actually allow an outside system to read and write for hourly requisitions, and who has to approve that.
