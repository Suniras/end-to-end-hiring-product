# 02-landscape: who else is doing this, and where they are weak

Phase 2. A teardown means going through a competitor's product properly rather than reading their
homepage and summarising it.

## Fountain first, and hardest

Fountain is the one that matters. Founded 2014, the leader in high-volume frontline hiring, customers
including UPS, Amazon DSP and Sweetgreen. Three products to understand: Fountain Hire (the frontline
recruiting system), Anna (an AI recruiter that runs structured screening conversations), and Cue
(launched April 2026, agents that run sourcing, screening and scheduling on their own).

The hard fact this project has to face: Fountain already built both the conversational screening agent
and the agentic layer. "We'll add AI agents to frontline hiring" is not a new idea in 2026. It is the
market leader's current product.

So for Fountain, find out what Cue and Anna actually do, what customers complain about, what the
product genuinely cannot do, where setup goes wrong, and what the pricing model punishes.

Then Paradox (Olivia), Harri, Phenom, HiredScore, and the underlying recruiting systems: Workday,
iCIMS, SuccessFactors.

## Competitive alternatives, not just competitors

April Dunford's point: a buyer's alternatives include doing nothing and carrying on as they are. For
frontline retail, the real incumbent is often a store manager doing it by hand with a stack of paper
applications. That is a competitor and it usually wins.

Also map what any real product has to sit alongside: the recruiting system, background checks, and
workforce scheduling.

## Sources that beat marketing pages

G2 and Capterra reviews, commentary from firms that do the implementations, job adverts that reveal
what a competitor is building next, Gartner coverage, and best of all people who have actually used
these systems.

Anything a competitor says about itself is an assumption, never verified.
