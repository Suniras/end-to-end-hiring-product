# Product Scope and Target Market

- Focus: end-to-end hiring system for large US retailers (2B+ revenue)
- Primary pain: high-volume seasonal hiring (holiday season), slow handoffs, compliance burden
- Target examples: Walmart, Target, Amazon, Flipkart, Reliance
- Declining hire volumes (450K in 2022 → ~350K in 2023) not a blocker
  - Driven by economic pressure and tighter compliance rules, not reduced need for the platform
- Core value prop: handle 4,000 hires in 90 days with seamless step-to-step transitions

# Hiring Funnel: 20-Step Framework

- Skeleton of 20 steps reviewed and approved; no need to overthink
- Priority: identify which steps are most critical and solve those first (top needle-movers in first 30–60 days)
- Key distinction to map: which steps rely on existing systems vs. which are just email/phone communication
- Step 0 and Step 20 (start/end): ensure no data loss or value drop-off between handoffs
- Handoff pain is a core problem to solve across the funnel

# Compliance, Connectors, and Edge Cases

- Two main compliance requirements flagged: I-9 verification and e-verification
  - Third-party tools (e.g., GreenLight) handle compliance checks; integrate via connector, not replacement
- Strategy: build connectors to existing established systems rather than replacing them
  - Retailers already have entrenched infrastructure; seamless integration is the sell
- Workday used at Target for end-to-end HR (recruitment, training, appraisals, payroll); focus only on the hiring layer
- Edge case: flagged candidates reapplying across different store locations
  - Proposed solution: central candidate database with biometric or government ID matching
  - Treat as exception-handling bucket; build a blacklist/whitelist knowledge base

# Application Ingestion and ATS

- Open question: how to capture new applications and migrate existing ones into the platform
  - New applicants: direct connector from job portals to the app
  - Existing applications: need to understand how Target’s central HQ database is structured
- Outsourcing hiring to third parties is expensive; most large retailers keep it in-house
  - Worth researching: what % of hiring is outsourced, and what third-party candidate pools look like
  - Opportunity: plug into a pre-screened third-party candidate pool at the shortlisting stage
