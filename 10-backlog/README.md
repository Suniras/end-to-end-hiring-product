# 10-backlog: turning the PRD into work

Phase S3. Epics broken into stories, each with acceptance criteria.

## The words

An **epic** is a large chunk of capability, too big to build in one go. A **story** is a single piece of
work a developer can finish and someone can check. **Acceptance criteria** are the conditions that
decide whether it is done.

## Who writes what

Suniras writes the acceptance criteria, before the work starts, and they have to be testable. This is
not delegatable. Acceptance criteria are where scope actually lives. If they are vague, scope is vague,
and "done" becomes a matter of opinion at the end of a sprint when nobody wants to argue.

Testable means someone could read the criterion and reach a yes or no without asking you what you
meant.

## What goes in here

Epics, stories, acceptance criteria, and sprint records. Keep the sprint records, because in six months
the question "when did we decide that" comes up and the answer is usually in them.

## The gate

Could someone else judge whether a story is done, from your criteria alone, without asking you?
