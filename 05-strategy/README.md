# 05-strategy: what we are doing, and what we are deliberately not

Phase 5. Strategy means choosing. A goal is not a strategy. "Become the leading frontline hiring
platform" is a wish with no decisions in it.

## What this phase produces

**A guiding policy.** The overall approach that explains why the specific choices below hang together.

**A beachhead.** Two parts, and both are needed. First, which narrow group of customers we go after
first. Second, which slice of the lifecycle: attract, hire, onboard, or activate. All four are real
bottlenecks and all four are in the long-term vision, but v1 is one of them. Say which, and say why
that one earns the right to the others afterwards.

**Explicit non-goals.** Things we are choosing not to build, written down next to the things we are.
Non-goals that only live in someone's head are not decisions, and they get quietly reversed.

**Positioning,** in April Dunford's sense: what alternatives a buyer is really comparing us to, what we
uniquely do, and who cares about that.

**The AI boundary.** Where agents create real value, where they make no difference, and where they
create legal or trust problems bigger than the benefit. Deciding "no AI here" in a particular step is a
strategic statement, not a gap in the product.

## The trap to avoid

We have NuPlay and NuAnchor already, so there is constant pull toward finding a problem shaped like
them. Also: Fountain already ships AI agents for this. Any strategy that amounts to catching up to Cue
and Anna is a losing one. When a direction gets proposed, the question is what it does that Fountain
doesn't, and "ours is better" is not an answer without a mechanism.

## The gate

Can you name three things we are deliberately not building, and defend each one?
