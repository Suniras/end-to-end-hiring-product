What it does

An HR team runs this across five systems. This runs all twenty steps.

A person applies on a Saturday night. Somebody has to screen them, book them, interview them, decide, offer, chase the silence, order a check, wait on a county court, take an I-9, clear E-Verify, provision a badge, place a first shift, and then notice on the Monday whether they turned up. Today that is a recruiter, a store manager, a coordinator, an applicant tracking system, a payroll system and a spreadsheet.

Both companies who sell into this market own the steps that happen inside their own product. Neither owns the gaps between them, and the gaps are where people disappear.

The two moments

The parts nobody else catches.

Everything above is table stakes and we should be judged on it. These two are the reason to look twice.

One

He scored 79. He is also not eligible for rehire.

Terminated for cause at another store in 2024. He spelled his name differently this time and every contact detail is new. To a system that starts clean he is a brand-new applicant.

We caught him because when a retailer leaves their old system we import exactly one list. Everything else can start from a cutover date. A do-not-hire list cannot.

Two

A mismatch is usually an admin error, and this is how retailers break the law by accident.

A new hire's E-Verify check comes back mismatched. She is a US citizen who hyphenated her surname after marrying, and the government record still has the old one. She contests it, which she is entitled to do.

While she contests, nothing adverse is lawful. Not termination, not suspension, not delayed training, and not quietly leaving her off the rota. Her store manager tried twice. The product stopped him both times and told him why.
