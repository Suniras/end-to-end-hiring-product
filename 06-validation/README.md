# 06-validation: proving it before engineering commits

Phase 6. Marty Cagan's four risks. The point of this phase is to find out which of these is fatal
while it is still cheap to change your mind.

**Value risk.** Will anyone actually want this enough to change what they do today? The most common
way products die.

**Usability risk.** Can the people who have to use it actually use it? For this project that means a
store manager with no training and a candidate on a phone in a noisy break room.

**Feasibility risk.** Can we build it, with the people and time we have, against the real constraints?
Real-time voice has a hard limit on delay. Connecting to a customer's recruiting system is harder than
it sounds.

**Business viability risk.** Does it work for Nurix? Pricing, support cost, legal exposure, sales
motion, and whether it fits what the company can actually sell.

## What goes in here

Prototypes, demand tests, and technical spikes run against real integration constraints rather than
imagined ones. Write down what you expected before you run each test, so the result can actually
surprise you.

## The strongest evidence in enterprise

A customer who commits to deploy and to be named. Not a customer who says the problem is real and the
demo looks good. Interest is free. Commitment costs something, which is why it means something.

## The gate

What is your single strongest piece of evidence that someone will pay for this?
